Neural-network parametrization: next follow-ups

Four directions to revisit after the notes on initialization, feature learning, normalization, and optimizer geometry.

Reading list · 24 September 2026

1. Attention-specific scaling

Make the meaning of “increasing width” explicit in a Transformer: model dimension, head dimension, number of heads, and MLP width need not grow together. Work through attention-logit scaling and token-to-token covariance, then identify which limits preserve nontrivial attention behavior. Increasing the number of heads and increasing the dimension of one head can lead to different limiting descriptions.

Possible note: derive one attention head from its query, key, and value projections, then compare two ways of making multi-head attention wider. Build on the NTK and normalization notes.

Start with: Jiri Hron, Yasaman Bahri, Jascha Sohl-Dickstein, and Roman Novak. Infinite attention: NNGP and NTK for deep attention networks. ICML 2020.

2. Unit scaling and u-μP

Connect width-independent learning dynamics to the numerical scales of stored weights, activations, and gradients. u-μP combines maximal-update parametrization with unit scaling, aiming to simplify hyperparameter conventions and make low-precision training easier. It is a practical continuation of the current scaling notes.

Possible note: rewrite a μP linear layer in unit-scaled coordinates, tracking forward and backward factors and the optimizer together. Explain why well-scaled real-valued dynamics and a suitable floating-point range are separate requirements. Continue from equivalent optimizer coordinates.

Start with: Charlie Blake et al. u-μP: The Unit-Scaled Maximal Update Parametrization. 2024, revised 2025.

3. Finite-width effects and joint width–depth limits

Study when a width-first approximation stops describing a deep finite network. A controlled mean can coexist with large fluctuations. For the ReLU MLPs studied by Hanin and Rolnick, the sum of reciprocal layer widths controls activation-length fluctuations; at constant width this becomes the depth-to-width ratio.

Possible note: expand the existing finite-width discussion by comparing fixed depth, depth proportional to width, and residual architectures. Separate fluctuations across random networks from the spread of singular values within one network, studied in dynamical isometry.

Start with: Boris Hanin and David Rolnick. How to Start Training: The Effect of Initialization and Architecture. NeurIPS 2018.

4. Compute-optimal scaling laws

Add the resource-allocation question: for a fixed training-compute budget, how should model size and training tokens grow together? Compare Kaplan et al. with Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla), including their fitted assumptions and experimental protocols. Keep this question distinct from how initialization and learning rates should scale with model dimensions.

Possible note: derive an optimal model/data allocation from a simple fitted loss law and compute constraint. Then explain how training horizon, tuning quality, and architecture affect comparisons. Connect this to CompleteP's compute-efficiency discussion.

Start with: Jared Kaplan et al. Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (2020), followed by Jordan Hoffmann et al. Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (2022).
